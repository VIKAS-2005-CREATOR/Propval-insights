Architectural Decision: Why Tree Ensembles Over Linear Models?

Earlier iterations explored baseline linear algorithms (Ordinary Least Squares, Ridge, and Lasso regression). During rigorous validation on the Mumbai real estate dataset, linear approaches were systematically outperformed by tree-based ensembles. Three fundamental structural characteristics of the Mumbai property market explain this outcome:

1. Non-Linear Geographic Scaling

In Mumbai, locality acts as a non-linear multiplier rather than a simple additive intercept. A 1,200 sq ft unit in Worli or Bandra West commands ₹50,000 to ₹75,000 per sq ft, whereas the identical area in Vasai or Dombivli trades at ₹7,000 to ₹10,000 per sq ft. Linear models fail to capture this location-specific square-foot slope without extensive manual interaction engineering.

2. Complex Higher-Order Interactions

Property attributes interact strongly: a high floor rise combined with transit proximity and ready-to-move status yields a disproportionate premium in South and Western Mumbai that does not exist in peripheral developments. Decision trees recursively segment feature spaces to automatically model these interactions.

3. Resilience to Extreme Luxury Outliers

Mumbai features ultra-high-net-worth properties reaching ₹50+ Crores. In standard linear regression, squared error losses cause these extreme points to dominate coefficient optimization, skewing everyday estimates for 1-BHK and 2-BHK apartments. Tree splits partition extreme observations into isolated leaves, preserving precision for mainstream housing.

Synthesis: Retaining only Gradient Boosting and Random Forest streamlines our ML runtime, eliminates redundant linear model overhead, and delivers 86.5% Test R² accuracy with complete interpretability.

Validation Protocol & Prediction Intervals

80/20 Independent Split & 5-Fold CV

The dataset of 52,160 listings was partitioned into an 80% training set and a 20% held-out test set. Hyperparameters were tuned strictly on the training set using 5-fold cross-validation, with the test set reserved exclusively for unbiased evaluation.

Logarithmic Target Transformation

Target prices were transformed using log(price) during training. This stabilizes heteroskedastic residual variance across different budget categories and ensures that percentage errors remain uniform from suburban to luxury segments.

90% Empirical Confidence Bounds

Prediction intervals are calculated from the empirical distribution of test residuals at the 5th and 95th percentiles (multipliers: 0.70x to 1.45x for well-represented micro-markets; 0.57x to 1.72x for sparse regions), providing honest risk boundaries.
